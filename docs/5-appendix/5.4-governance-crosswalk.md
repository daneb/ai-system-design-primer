# 5.4 Governance crosswalk: OWASP, FINOS, EU AI Act

Every deep dive ends with a "Risk and controls" table: the risk, the reference it maps to, the
control, and the evidence an auditor would ask for. This page pulls those references together, so a
security, risk or audit reviewer can start from the framework they know and find the primer sections
that address it.

> This page is engineering guidance, not legal advice. Regulatory dates and scope change; confirm with
> your legal and compliance teams.

## The frameworks, in one line each

- **OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications (2025).** Ten security risks for applications built on language
  models, LLM01–LLM10. The shared vocabulary for threat modelling and red-team scope.
- **OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications (2026).** Published December 2025; ten risks that appear when
  AI systems plan, use tools, keep memory and talk to other agents, ASI01–ASI10. Adds to the LLM list,
  doesn't replace it.
- **FINOS AI Governance Framework (AIGF v2).** An open catalogue of AI risks and mitigations written by
  and for financial institutions, with a dedicated agentic catalogue (November 2025) and mappings to
  OWASP, NIST, ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act and SR 11-7.
- **EU AI Act.** Regulation by risk tier: prohibited practices, high-risk systems (Annex III uses such
  as credit scoring and employment), transparency duties, and rules for general-purpose AI models.
- **NIST AI Risk Management Framework.** A management loop: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage.
- **ISO/IEC 42001.** A certifiable AI management system standard.
- **Model risk management** (for example the US Federal Reserve's SR 11-7, and local equivalents):
  treat AI systems as models with documented purpose, validation, monitoring and an owner.
- **Data protection law** (GDPR, South Africa's POPIA and others): lawful basis, minimisation,
  retention, access and erasure for personal data in prompts, traces and memory.

## EU AI Act dates

| Date | What applies |
|---|---|
| 1 August 2024 | The Act enters into force |
| 2 February 2025 | Prohibited practices; AI literacy |
| 2 August 2025 | Rules for general-purpose AI models |
| 2 August 2026 | Most transparency duties (Art. 50); some watermarking duties moved to 2 December 2026 by the Digital Omnibus |
| 2 December 2027 | High-risk obligations for stand-alone (Annex III) systems, delayed from August 2026 by the Digital Omnibus |
| 2 August 2028 | High-risk obligations for AI in regulated products (Annex I), delayed from August 2027 |

The Digital Omnibus delay was provisionally agreed on 7 May 2026 and voted by the European Parliament
on 16 June 2026; confirm the final published text. Most of the systems in this primer (assistants,
document review, coding agents) aren't high-risk under Annex III; credit scoring and employment
decisions are.

## Using this with a review board

- **Start every AI system with a risk-tiering record:** use case, users, data, autonomy, and which rows
  above apply.
- **For each row that applies, bring the deep dive's "Evidence an auditor would ask for"** column: it's
  written as a checklist.
- **Revisit at every model or prompt change** ([4.7](../4-solved-designs/4.7-eval-and-release-pipeline.md)).

## Flashcards

Q: How does the OWASP Agentic Top 10 relate to the OWASP LLM Top 10?
A: It adds risks specific to agents (goal hijack, tool misuse, identity abuse, memory poisoning, inter-agent communication, cascading failures, rogue agents); the LLM list still applies.

Q: Which OWASP Agentic risk is the agent counterpart of prompt injection?
A: ASI01 Agent Goal Hijack.

Q: What is the FINOS AI Governance Framework?
A: An open catalogue of AI risks and mitigations by financial institutions, mapped to OWASP, NIST, ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act and SR 11-7, with an agentic risk catalogue since v2.

Q: When do EU AI Act high-risk obligations for Annex III systems apply after the Digital Omnibus?
A: 2 December 2027 (provisionally agreed in May 2026; confirm the published text).

Q: Which EU AI Act article covers record-keeping for high-risk systems, and which primer section supports it?
A: Article 12; observability and tracing (3.16).
