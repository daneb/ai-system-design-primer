# 5.5 Real-world architectures and engineering blogs

The original System Design Primer ends with how real companies built real systems. This page does the
same for AI systems: write-ups from teams who built and ran them, chosen because they say what went
wrong as well as what worked. Each one is cited in the primer section listed beside it.

## How to read these

- **Ask what problem forced the design.** Most of these architectures exist because a simpler one
  failed: cost, quality, latency, security or scale.
- **Look for the numbers.** Token use, success rates, prompt counts, latency. They're dated and specific
  to one system; use them as orders of magnitude, not targets ([5.1](5.1-numbers.md)).
- **Read the vendor posts as vendor posts.** Teams describe what they shipped. Independent studies
  (marked below) are where claims get tested.

## Flashcards

Q: What did Anthropic find explained most of its multi-agent research system's performance?
A: Token usage, about 80% of the variance on a browsing benchmark.

Q: What lesson does Klarna's customer-service reversal teach about measuring AI support?
A: Optimising for cost and containment lowered quality; measure resolution and customer outcomes.

Q: What did METR's 2025 trial show about perceived vs measured productivity?
A: Developers felt 20% faster with AI tools but were measured 19% slower.
