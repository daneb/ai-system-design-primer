# 2.1 Cost vs quality vs latency: deep dive

Reference material for [2.1 Cost vs quality vs latency](../2-trade-offs/2.1-cost-quality-latency.md). Read that first.
This page keeps the full treatment: provider survey, numbers, risk and controls, cloud mapping
and the complete source list.

## The trade-off

Every model call has a price, a quality and a wait time, and the levers that improve one usually worsen
another. A larger model or more reasoning raises quality, cost and latency together. A smaller model
cuts cost and latency and may cut quality. Unlike CAP, this isn't a theorem: it is a budget you
allocate, and good designs push the whole curve outwards (better quality at the same cost) rather than
just moving along it.

Google's Well-Architected guidance starts where this primer's [Method](../1-method/1-method.md) does:
turn business goals into explicit targets, then design to meet them, accepting that the design
delivering the performance you need may cost more
([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/perspectives/ai-ml/performance-optimization)). Anthropic adds
an order of work: first make the output good, then reduce latency, because optimising early hides what
top quality looks like ([Anthropic](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-latency)).

## Where cost and latency come from

- **Tokens.** You pay per input and output token. Output tokens are usually priced higher and are
  generated one at a time.
- **Output length drives latency.** OpenAI's rule of thumb: cutting output tokens by half cuts latency
  by about half, while cutting input tokens by half may save only 1–5%
  ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latency-optimization)).
- **Model size.** Larger models are slower per token and cost more per token.
- **Reasoning.** Models that think before answering spend extra tokens and time; providers expose this
  as a setting (Anthropic calls it *effort*), which makes it a cost-and-latency dial.
- **Number of calls.** Every sequential call adds a round trip; agents multiply calls and tokens
  ([1 Method](../1-method/1-method.md#step-2-back-of-the-envelope)).

Users feel two latencies: time to first token, and time to the complete answer. Streaming improves the
first without changing the second or the cost.

## The levers

| Lever | Cost | Latency | Quality risk | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Smaller model tier | ↓↓ | ↓ | Medium | All providers |
| Route each request to the cheapest adequate model | ↓ | ↓ | Depends on router accuracy | FrugalGPT; AWS; Azure |
| Shorter outputs (instructions, `max_tokens`) | ↓ | ↓↓ | Low if measured | OpenAI; Anthropic |
| Prompt caching of a repeated prefix | ↓↓ (cached part) | ↓ | None | [3.13](../3-components/3.13-caching.md) |
| Batch processing for work that can wait | ↓ (about 50%) | ↑↑ (hours) | None | All five providers |
| Streaming | — | ↓ perceived | None | OpenAI; Anthropic |
| Parallel calls for independent steps | — | ↓ | None | OpenAI |
| Fewer calls: combine steps | ↓ | ↓ | Low to medium | OpenAI |
| Lower reasoning effort | ↓ | ↓ | Medium | Anthropic |
| Don't use a model at all (hard-code, ordinary code) | ↓↓ | ↓↓ | None where it fits | OpenAI |
| Fine-tune or distil a smaller model | ↓ at volume | ↓ | Needs evals | [2.6](../2-trade-offs/2.6-prompt-rag-finetune.md) |

**Routing** deserves a closer look. Chen et al. showed that a learned cascade of models of different
prices could match the best single model's accuracy on their tasks at up to 98% lower cost
([FrugalGPT, TMLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176)). Clouds now sell this: AWS claims its
prompt router cuts cost by up to 30% without losing accuracy
([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/intelligent-prompt-routing)), and Azure's model router offers
cost, balanced and quality modes that pick the cheapest model within a quality band
([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/foundry/openai/concepts/model-router)). Treat
vendor figures as upper bounds measured on their workloads.

[Lab 2.1](../../labs/2.1-cost-quality-latency/README.md) shows why the router is the hard part: with a
10× price gap, a good router kept accuracy within one point of the large model at 64% of the cost; a
poor router at the same cost lost 3.4 points.

**Batch** is the cheapest lever when nobody is waiting: OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Azure and AWS all offer
roughly half price for asynchronous jobs that finish within hours
([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/message-batches-api)). Overnight document processing,
evaluation runs and back-fills belong there.

## How to decide

1. Set the three targets first: the quality bar (from evals), the latency budget (first token and
   total), and the cost ceiling per request or per month.
2. Find the best quality you can reach, ignoring cost.
3. Then walk down the levers above, re-running the eval suite after each change
   ([3.15](../3-components/3.15-evals.md)), and stop when a target would break.
4. Revisit when prices or models change: the right point on the curve moves every few months.

## Failure modes

- **Optimising cost before quality is known**, so nobody can tell what the savings cost.
- **Comparing models on price per token alone.** A cheaper model that needs longer prompts, more retries
  or more calls can cost more per task.
- **Unmeasured routing.** Savings show up on the bill immediately; quality loss shows up later in
  complaints.
- **Ignoring output length**, the biggest latency lever, while tuning everything else.
- **Synchronous calls for work nobody is waiting for.**

## Cloud mapping

| Lever | Azure | AWS | Google Cloud | OpenAI | Anthropic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Routing between models | Foundry model router | Bedrock Intelligent Prompt Routing | Vertex AI Model Optimizer (cost, quality or balanced) | — | — |
