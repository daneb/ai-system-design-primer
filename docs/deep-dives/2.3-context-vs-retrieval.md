## What providers say

| Provider | Position | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Anthropic | Under about 200,000 tokens (≈500 pages), you can include the whole knowledge base in the prompt; prompt caching makes this faster and cheaper. | Google | Gemini's long context "invites a more direct approach": provide all relevant information up front; context caching is the main optimisation; put the question at the end; if you don't need tokens, don't send them. | OpenAI | Long context windows plus automatic prompt caching; file search for retrieval. No first-party decision rule found. | (not found) |

## The research

- **Li et al. (Google DeepMind, EMNLP 2024).** Compared long context (LC) and RAG across public datasets
  with GPT-4o, GPT-3.5-Turbo and Gemini-1.5-Pro. With enough resources, LC beat RAG on average; RAG cost
  far less. *Self-Route* lets the model decide per query whether retrieved passages suffice and falls back
  to LC otherwise, keeping performance close to LC at much lower cost: 65% lower for Gemini-1.5-Pro and
  39% for GPT-4o (the paper's own token table implies about 62% for Gemini), with more than half of
  queries answered from retrieval alone.
- **Earlier work (Xu et al., 2023)** found the opposite, which Li et al. attribute to weaker models and
  shorter contexts. Results move with model generations; re-test on your models.
- **LaRA (2025)**: no single winner; the best choice depends on task, model and context length
  (via secondary sources).
- **Context rot and lost in the middle** ([3.3 deep dive](3.3-context-engineering.md)) are the
  reasons stuffing degrades with size even when it fits.

## Decision factors

| Factor | Favours stuffing | Favours retrieval |
|---|---|---|
| Corpus size | Fits easily (tens to low hundreds of thousands of tokens) | Larger than the window, or growing |
| Traffic | Busy enough to keep the cache warm | Sparse, or highly varied corpora per request |
| Question type | Whole-corpus reasoning, comparison, absence | Lookups needing a few passages |
| Freshness | Changes rarely | Changes daily |
| Access control | Every user may see everything | Per-user or per-role filtering |
| Evidence | Model citations acceptable | Passage-level citations required |
| Latency | Batch or tolerant | Interactive |
| Team | No retrieval stack yet | Retrieval stack exists |

## Risk and controls

| Risk | Reference | Control | Evidence an auditor would ask for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Users see content they aren't entitled to | OWASP LLM02 (sensitive information disclosure); data protection | Stuff only corpora every user may see; otherwise retrieve with permission filters ([3.4](3.4-retrieval-pipeline.md)) | Entitlement design; access tests |
| Stale answers from a cached snapshot | Model risk | Version the stuffed corpus; invalidate on change | Corpus version in traces |
| Unverifiable answers | Model risk; audit | Require citations; with stuffing, check cited text exists | Citation-accuracy evals ([3.15](3.15-evals.md)) |
| Cost spikes as the corpus grows | Operational | Budget alerts; size limit that triggers a move to retrieval | Cost dashboard |
