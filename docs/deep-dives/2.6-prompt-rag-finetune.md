# 2.6 Prompting vs RAG vs fine-tuning: deep dive

Reference material for [2.6 Prompting vs RAG vs fine-tuning](../2-trade-offs/2.6-prompt-rag-finetune.md). Read that first.
This page keeps the full treatment: provider survey, numbers, risk and controls, cloud mapping
and the complete source list.

## The trade-off

When a model's output isn't good enough, there are three main levers. **Prompting** changes the
instructions and examples you send. **Retrieval (RAG)** adds the right information to the prompt at
request time ([3.4](../3-components/3.4-retrieval-pipeline.md)). **Fine-tuning** changes the model's
weights by training it further on your examples ([3.18](../3-components/3.18-model-adaptation.md)).

They trade iteration speed against how deeply they change behaviour. A prompt change takes minutes and
is easy to undo. A retrieval pipeline takes days to build but updates as fast as you can re-index. A
fine-tune takes hours to days per run, needs a labelled dataset, and becomes a model you must
version, evaluate and retrain.

## Two questions, not a ladder

OpenAI's accuracy guide frames the choice as two separate problems rather than one sequence
([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/optimizing-llm-accuracy)):

- **Does the model lack knowledge?** The facts aren't in its training data, are out of date, or are
  private. That is a *context* problem: fix it with retrieval.
- **Does the model behave wrongly?** Inconsistent format, wrong tone, steps not followed reliably. That
  is a *behaviour* problem: fix it with prompting first, then fine-tuning if prompting plateaus.

The two can stack: some systems need both.

Every provider agrees on where to start:

- **Prompting first.** Anthropic lists the reasons: prompting is far faster to iterate, cheaper, needs
  few or no labelled examples, and avoids the risk of the model losing general ability when its weights
  change ([Anthropic](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/intro-to-prompting)).
- **RAG for knowledge.** AWS recommends starting with RAG for question answering over your own
  documents and fine-tuning when the model must learn a new task; it notes that fine-tuned models can't
  cite sources and may hallucinate more when answering questions
  ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/retrieval-augmented-generation-options/rag-vs-fine-tuning.html)).
- **Fine-tuning for behaviour prompts can't pin down.** Google points to tasks such as classification
  into fixed labels, consistent structured output and domain-specific conventions
  ([Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/gemini-supervised-tuning));
  Thoughtworks reserves fine-tuning for when evals remain unsatisfactory after good retrieval
  ([martinfowler.com](https://www.martinfowler.com/articles/gen-ai-patterns/)).

## Why fine-tuning is a poor way to add facts

Two 2024 studies explain the consensus:

- Microsoft researchers compared unsupervised fine-tuning with RAG for injecting knowledge, including
  facts newer than the model's training data. RAG won consistently, and fine-tuning only learned new
  facts when shown many paraphrases of each ([Ovadia et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.15/)).
- Google researchers found that fine-tuning examples containing new facts are learned much more slowly
  than familiar ones, and that once learned they make the model more prone to hallucinate. Their
  conclusion: models get facts from pre-training; fine-tuning teaches them to use what they know
  ([Gekhman et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.444)).

## Comparison

| | Prompting | RAG | Fine-tuning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixes | Behaviour, format, reasoning steps | Missing, private or changing knowledge | Behaviour prompting can't make consistent; cost and latency at scale |
| Time to change | Minutes | Minutes to re-index; days to build | Hours to days per training run |
| Data needed | A few examples | The documents | A labelled dataset that matches production inputs |
| Keeps up with changing facts | No | Yes | No: retrain |
| Can cite sources | No | Yes | No |
| Cost shape | Per-call tokens (longer prompts cost more) | Indexing plus per-call tokens | Training plus hosting; shorter prompts per call |
| Main risk | Long, brittle prompts | Retrieval misses ([3.4](../3-components/3.4-retrieval-pipeline.md)) | Lost general ability; more hallucination if trained on new facts |

Fine-tuning has one more use: training a smaller, cheaper model to do one narrow task as well as a
larger one, which cuts latency and cost per call. Google cites customers who moved to smaller models this
way ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/transform/top-five-gen-ai-tuning-use-cases-gemini-hundreds-of-orgs)).
That is a cost decision ([2.1](../2-trade-offs/2.1-cost-quality-latency.md)), and it only pays at volume.

## Combining them

The levers are additive. A fine-tuned model can still use retrieval: AWS points to RAFT (UC Berkeley),
which fine-tunes a model specifically to answer from retrieved documents. The order of work still
holds: get the prompt and retrieval right, measure with evals ([3.15](../3-components/3.15-evals.md)),
and fine-tune only against a failing eval that the other levers can't fix.

## Failure modes

- **Fine-tuning to teach facts.** Slow to learn, can't cite, goes stale, and can raise hallucination.
- **Fine-tuning before measuring.** Without an eval baseline you can't show the fine-tune helped.
- **Treating RAG as a behaviour fix.** Retrieved context doesn't make the model follow a format.
- **Forgetting the upkeep.** Each base-model upgrade means retraining and re-evaluating a fine-tune;
  prompts and retrieval usually carry over with re-evaluation only.

## Risk and controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Training data contains personal or confidential information that the model can later reveal | Classify, minimise and approve fine-tuning datasets; prefer retrieval with access control for sensitive knowledge |
| A fine-tuned model is a new model to validate | Treat it as a model release: documented data, evals against the base model, sign-off |
| Answers can't be traced to sources | Use RAG where traceability matters; fine-tuned models can't cite |
