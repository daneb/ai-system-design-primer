# 3.7 Model layer: deep dive

Reference material for [3.7 Model layer](../3-components/3.7-model-layer.md). Read that first.

## Selection: where the providers agree

- **OpenAI** sets the order explicitly: optimise for accuracy until you hit the target, then keep it
  with the cheapest, fastest model; start with the most capable model, log its responses, then compare
  smaller models zero- or few-shot or fine-tune one on the logged data. Its worked example reached all
  targets with a fine-tuned small model trained on 1,000 labelled examples
  ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/model-selection)).
- **Anthropic** advises making the output good first, then reducing latency
  ([2.1](2.1-cost-quality-latency.md)), and offers an *effort* setting that trades reasoning depth for
  speed and cost.
- **Google, AWS and Microsoft** route the same decision through their model catalogues and routers
  ([3.1](3.1-ai-gateway-and-router.md)).

## Long context

Liu et al. showed models use information at the start and end of a long input better than the middle
([TACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/)). Chroma's 2025 report tested 18 models and found
performance grows less reliable as input length grows, even on simple tasks, and that distractors
make it worse; it also notes that needle-in-a-haystack scores overstate real-world reliability
([Hong et al.](https://trychroma.com/research/context-rot)). NoLiMa found sharp drops when the
question and the needed fact share no words ([arXiv 2502.05167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167)).
Chroma sells retrieval infrastructure, which is worth knowing when weighing its framing; the effect
itself replicates across independent studies.

## Versions and lifecycle by provider

- **Anthropic** lists lifecycle stages and retirement dates on its deprecations page and gives every
  model a specific ID; dated IDs are pinned snapshots (secondary sources state newer dateless IDs are
  also pinned; not verified here).
- **OpenAI** documents model families and dated snapshots and recommends pinning for consistent
  behaviour (via secondary sources quoting its compatibility guidance; not verified here).
- **Google** labels models stable, legacy stable or retired; a stable version typically ships with a
  retirement date, and new access is blocked one month before it. Auto-updated aliases always point to
  the latest stable model ([Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/learn/model-versions)).
- **AWS** uses Active, Legacy and End-of-Life. A model stays on Bedrock at least 12 months before EOL
  and at least 6 months in Legacy; Bedrock's dates can differ from the model provider's, and Legacy
  models can't be newly fine-tuned ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-lifecycle.html)).
- **Microsoft** sets an upgrade policy per deployment: upgrade within two weeks of a new default
  version, upgrade at retirement (the default when unset), or never, in which case the deployment
  stops working at retirement ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/ai-foundry/openai/how-to/working-with-models)).

The cloud dates differ from the model makers' dates. Track the platform you actually call.

## Failure modes and antipatterns

- **Model IDs scattered through the code** instead of one configuration per route.
- **Retrying a "model not found" error** as if it were transient. It means retired or misspelled.
- **Comparing models on different prompts.** Prompts tuned for one model often need changes for another.
- **Ignoring tokenizer differences**, which change cost and context fit after an upgrade.
- **Unsupported parameters after migration** (different reasoning or sampling controls) failing silently.

## Risk and controls

| Risk | Reference | Control | Evidence an auditor would ask for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model changes without review | Model risk management; change management | Pinned versions; upgrade policy set explicitly; eval gate before change | Model inventory with versions; change records with eval results |
| Model retired with no replacement ready | Operational resilience | Track lifecycle dates per platform; tested fallback model | Lifecycle register; fallback test results |
| Chosen model below quality bar | Model risk | Eval sized to the decision; confidence bounds in selection | Eval report with intervals |
| Third-party model terms or data use unsuitable | Third-party risk | Approved model catalogue at the gateway ([3.1](3.1-ai-gateway-and-router.md)) | Catalogue; vendor assessments |
